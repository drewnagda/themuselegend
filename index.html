The Roots of Muse

John Legend × Pharrell Williams.
The music behind the album.

I The Spark

It started with
someone else’s record.

Pharrell produced every song on Clipse’s comeback album, Let God Sort Em Out. The opener, “The Birds Don’t Sing,” carries a hook sung by John Legend. Pharrell loved hearing John’s voice on what he had written.

Then came the phone call. Pharrell lives in Paris; John lives in Los Angeles. The call landed around 5 a.m. LA time, and John didn’t pick up. When he called back, Pharrell had one question.

“I called him after I woke up and he’s like, ‘What are you doing for your next album?’”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

“He was like, man, we should just do a whole album together.”

John Legend The Breakfast Club, Oct 8, 2026

Track 05 · the first song

Daylight

Not long after the call, Pharrell sent over a song he’d written with singer-songwriter Samm Henshaw. John cut it at home in LA around Christmas 2024. It became the lead single, and in the official bio, “the song that started it all.”

“If this is what the album can sound like, then we are really in for something.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

The first sessions followed in Paris in January 2025. Universal Music Canada

II The Brief

Black James Bond,
but with 808 drums.

Pharrell arrived with a vision. Classic and cinematic on top, a modern hip-hop backbeat underneath.

“He talked about making it cinematic, black James Bond, but with 808 drums.”

John Legend The Breakfast Club, Oct 8, 2026

“I really want to make something classic that reminds people of the classics that we all love.”

John Legend, recalling Pharrell’s pitch, The Deja Vu Show, Sep 25, 2026

“The 808s on the bottom, but all those classic instruments on the top, so strings and orchestral arrangements and horns.”

John Legend Elvis Duran and the Morning Show, Oct 9, 2026

The vocal note, as 106.7 Lite FM put it

“Sing less,
talk it more.”

John’s instinct is the big voice: vibrato, a rich and weighty tone. Pharrell pushed the other way, toward restraint, talk-singing certain lines in the conversational register of Frank Sinatra or Nat King Cole.

“There were certain songs he was like, sing less and kind of talk it.”

John Legend 106.7 Lite FM, Oct 7, 2026

“My instinct is to sing big. I’ve got vibrato and a rich, weighty tone.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

Where Most of Muse was made in Pharrell’s studio at the back of Louis Vuitton’s Paris headquarters, down the hall from where he designs as Men’s Creative Director, with a piano in the corner and a view of the Seine. Roc Nation · The Breakfast Club

III The Muses

“Our ancestors
are our muses.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

Four names keep coming up when John talks about this album. The artists are his words. The song beside each name is our pairing, marked our pick.

I

Nina Simone

Listen Feeling Good our pick

Her name comes up again and again in his Muse interviews. Our pick for her slot leans into the big, cinematic orchestra the record is built around.

“Nina Simone is a big influence, Nat King Cole, Marvin Gaye.”

John Legend Elvis Duran and the Morning Show, Oct 9, 2026
II

Nat King Cole

Listen Nature Boy our pick

Pharrell steered John away from singing big, toward talk-singing lines “in the conversational register of Frank Sinatra or Nat King Cole.” “Nature Boy” is that register at its most intimate.

“One of Pharrell’s ideas was to be more conversational with my delivery, and it really works for these songs.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026
III

Marvin Gaye

Listen Trouble Man our pick

Pharrell’s pitch was cinematic from day one. Marvin’s film-score record is our pick for the strings-and-horns, soundtrack side of the brief.

“Some of them are ancestors now, like Nina Simone and Marvin Gaye and Nat King Cole.”

John Legend The Breakfast Club, Oct 8, 2026
IV

Frank Sinatra

Listen Fly Me To The Moon our pick

The other half of that conversational register, and one of the “classics and standards” John says they talked about while making the record, laid over hip-hop drums.

“We talked about Nina Simone. We talked about Nat King Cole. We talked about Marvin Gaye. We talked about Frank Sinatra.”

John Legend 106.7 Lite FM, Oct 7, 2026

Also in the room

Aretha · Stevie · Donny Hathaway

“These are artists that when I’m in the studio, they’re in my head.”

John Legend The Deja Vu Show, Sep 25, 2026
