I The Spark

It started with
someone else’s record.

Pharrell produced every song on Clipse’s comeback album, Let God Sort Em Out. The opener, “The Birds Don’t Sing,” carries a hook sung by John Legend. Pharrell loved hearing John’s voice on what he had written.

Then came the phone call. Pharrell lives in Paris; John lives in Los Angeles. The call landed around 5 a.m. LA time, and John didn’t pick up. When he called back, Pharrell had one question.

“I called him after I woke up and he’s like, ‘What are you doing for your next album?’”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

“He was like, man, I really want to just do a whole album with you.”

John Legend Mina SayWhat, Sep 28, 2026

Track 05 · the first song

Daylight

Not long after the call, Pharrell sent over a song he’d written with singer-songwriter Samm Henshaw. John cut it at home in LA around Christmas 2024. It became the lead single, and in the official bio, “the song that started it all.”

“If this is what the album can sound like, then we are really in for something.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

The first sessions followed in Paris in January 2025. Mina SayWhat

II The Brief

Black James Bond,
but with 808 drums.

Pharrell arrived with a vision. Classic and cinematic on top, a modern hip-hop backbeat underneath.

“He talked about making it cinematic, black James Bond, but with 808 drums.”

John Legend The Breakfast Club, Oct 8, 2026

“I really want to make something classic that reminds people of the classics that we all love.”

John Legend, recalling Pharrell’s pitch, The Deja Vu Show, Sep 25, 2026

“The 808s on the bottom, but all those classic instruments on the top, so strings and orchestral arrangements and horns.”

John Legend Elvis Duran and the Morning Show, Oct 9, 2026

The vocal note, as 106.7 Lite FM put it

“Sing less,
talk it more.”

John’s instinct is the big voice: vibrato, a rich and weighty tone. Pharrell pushed the other way, toward restraint, talk-singing certain lines in the conversational register of Frank Sinatra or Nat King Cole.

“There were certain songs he was like, sing less and kind of talk it.”

John Legend 106.7 Lite FM, Oct 7, 2026

“My instinct is to sing big. I’ve got vibrato and a rich, weighty tone.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

Where Most of Muse was made in Pharrell’s studio at the back of Louis Vuitton’s Paris headquarters, down the hall from where he designs as Men’s Creative Director, with a piano in the corner and a view of the Seine. Roc Nation · The Breakfast Club

III The Muses

“Our ancestors
are our muses.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026

Four names come up every time John talks about this album. The artists are his words. The specific song in each slot is our pairing for the playlist, marked our pick.

I

Nina Simone

In the playlist Feeling Good our pick

Her name comes up in every Muse interview we read. Our pick for her slot leans into the big, cinematic orchestra the record is built around.

“Nina Simone is a big influence, Nat King Cole, Marvin Gaye.”

John Legend Elvis Duran and the Morning Show, Oct 9, 2026
II

Nat King Cole

In the playlist Nature Boy our pick

Pharrell steered John away from singing big, toward talk-singing lines “in the conversational register of Frank Sinatra or Nat King Cole.” “Nature Boy” is that register at its most intimate.

“One of Pharrell’s ideas was to be more conversational with my delivery, and it really works for these songs.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026
III

Marvin Gaye

In the playlist Trouble Man our pick

Pharrell’s pitch was cinematic from day one. Marvin’s film-score record is our pick for the strings-and-horns, soundtrack side of the brief.

“Some of them are ancestors now, like Nina Simone and Marvin Gaye and Nat King Cole.”

John Legend The Breakfast Club, Oct 8, 2026
IV

Frank Sinatra

In the playlist Fly Me To The Moon our pick

The other half of that conversational register, and one of the “classics and standards” John says they talked about while making the record, laid over hip-hop drums.

“We talked about Nina Simone. We talked about Nat King Cole. We talked about Marvin Gaye. We talked about Frank Sinatra.”

John Legend 106.7 Lite FM, Oct 7, 2026

Also in the room

Aretha · Stevie · Donny Hathaway

“These are artists that when I’m in the studio, they’re in my head.”

John Legend The Deja Vu Show, Sep 25, 2026

IV Track by Track

Sixteen songs.
What’s on the record.

All sixteen are out. Under each song: who wrote it, who produced it and who plays on it, read from the official Apple Music credits on release night, plus everything John and the label have said about it. No sample or interpolation is credited on any track.

Side A
  1. 01

    Muse feat. Pharrell Williams

    The title track opens the album and “gives Muse its spine”: a tumultuous relationship, sketched from the romance of Paris and high art. The name reaches back to the nine Muses of Greek mythology, who governed music, science, literature and the arts.

    “We conjured a character we could write about, freely.”

    John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026
    Written by
    Pharrell Williams and John Stephens
    Produced by
    Pharrell Williams
    In the credits
    John on piano; Pharrell on additional vocals; horns arranged by Terrace Martin, who also plays alto sax.

    Listen on Spotify

    Source: Apple Music credits · Roc Nation

  2. 02

    Pray For Ya

    The first taste of Muse happened on a runway: its “rollicking, gospel-tinged groove” soundtracked Louis Vuitton’s Fall-Winter 2026 Men’s show. John was in the room when the orchestra started playing chords he recognized, then the beat dropped. One of the first songs out of the sessions.

    “I hear the orchestra playing some chords, and then I’m like, I recognize those chords.”

    John Legend Elvis Duran and the Morning Show, Oct 9, 2026
    Written by
    Pharrell Williams, John Stephens, Kawan Prather and Samm Henshaw
    Produced by
    Pharrell Williams
    In the credits
    Usher sings background vocals, next to Pharrell and Natalie Imani. Quintin Gulledge on Rhodes and Hammond organ; strings arranged by Larry Gold and Steven Tirpak; horns arranged by Terrace Martin.

    Listen on Spotify

    Source: Apple Music credits · Roc Nation · Elvis Duran and the Morning Show

  4. 04

    Her feat. Pharrell Williams

    Pulls from the doo-wop and gospel John’s father raised him on (his dad sang and played drums; his mother directed the church choir), distilled into a tender groove about falling head over heels.

    “It’s very nostalgic, very romantic. There’s a sweetness to this record. It’s a portrait of infatuation.”

    John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026
    Written by
    Pharrell Williams, John Stephens, Kawan Prather and Samm Henshaw
    Produced by
    Pharrell Williams and John Stephens
    In the credits
    John is credited as additional producer here, beside Pharrell. Quintin Gulledge on electric guitar; strings conducted by Larry Gold and Matt Jones.

    Listen on Spotify

    Source: Apple Music credits · Roc Nation

  5. 05

    Daylight

    The song that started it all. Pharrell wrote it with Samm Henshaw and sent it over; John cut it at home in LA around Christmas 2024. Credited writers: Pharrell Williams, Samm Henshaw, Wayne Hector, John Stephens. No sample or interpolation credited.

    “If this is what the album can sound like, then we are really in for something.”

    John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026
    Written by
    Pharrell Williams, Samm Henshaw, Wayne Hector and John Stephens
    Produced by
    Pharrell Williams
    In the credits
    The background choir is the room itself: John, Samm Henshaw, Kawan Prather, Terrace Martin, Quintin Gulledge, Jessyca Wilson and Natalie Imani. Samm Henshaw also plays acoustic guitar.

    Listen on Spotify

    Source: Roc Nation · Apple Music credits · Genius

  6. 06

    Get Back

    Where the album’s central idea is most audible: “smoky, cinematic soul built on big horns and strings,” the Black James Bond flair rendered in full.

  3. 12

    Bodies On The Floor feat. Clipse

    John wrote the lyric with Samm Henshaw while watching the crime drama MobLand, letting its gang-war language bleed into a toxic, magnetic love story. His own reference point: “Again” and “Another Again” from his second album, Once Again. And Clipse, whose record started all this, return.

    “I wanted to write songs filled with tension and friction.”

The players

The same hands run through the whole record. Strings: Larry Gold (conductor and arranger), Matt Jones (conductor and arranger) and Steven Tirpak (arranger). Horns: Terrace Martin (arranger, saxophones), Danny Janklow (saxophones, flute), Brandyn Philips (trumpet, flugelhorn) and Devon Taylor (trombone, tuba, sousaphone). Keys and guitar: Quintin Gulledge. Voices: Jessyca Wilson and Natalie Imani. Every song is produced by Pharrell Williams, and no sample or interpolation is credited anywhere on the album.

Credits as listed on Apple Music. Tracklist, titles and sides as printed on johnlegend.com

V 20 Years

Two decades of friendship.
One album.

“This is the first time in more than two decades of friendship that the two have gone all-in on a project together.” Roc Nation

  1. The beginning

    Introduced by Kanye

    Legend and Pharrell first met through Kanye West. Pharrell was already a towering producer and style icon; Legend and West were still largely unknown.

    Roc Nation

  2. 2008

    “It’s Over”

    A song for Evolver, John’s third album, featuring Kanye West, with Pharrell among the writers.

    Roc Nation · Wikipedia

  3. 2013

    “Aim High”

    Written and produced by Pharrell for Love in the Future.

    Roc Nation · Wikipedia

  4. Then

    The hook

    John sings the hook on “The Birds Don’t Sing,” the opener of Clipse’s comeback album Let God Sort Em Out, produced entirely by Pharrell.

    Roc Nation · Mina SayWhat · Wikipedia

  5. Late 2024

    The 5 a.m. call

    “A few months after we finished that Clipse record,” Pharrell calls from Paris before dawn, LA time: what are you doing for your next album? Then he sends “Daylight,” and John cuts it at home in LA around Christmas 2024.

    Roc Nation · The Breakfast Club

  6. January 2025

    Paris

    Sessions start in Pharrell’s studio inside Louis Vuitton’s Paris headquarters, then continue over Zoom and on more trips. Most of the music was ready by the end of 2025.

    Mina SayWhat · Roc Nation · The Breakfast Club

  7. Sep 8, 2026

    The announcement

    Muse is announced: sixteen songs, produced in full by Pharrell Williams.

    Billboard · Universal Music Canada · CBS Mornings

  8. Oct 23, 2026

    Muse

    Out Friday on Republic Records.

    WTFM · Universal Music Canada

“A lot of people would have imagined we’d done something like this already, but we hadn’t. It felt past due.”

John Legend Roc Nation, Oct 7, 2026
